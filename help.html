💚 How to use MoneyMate NZ

A step-by-step guide for the website and Android app. Track income, expenses, budgets, savings goals and bills. Import supported NZD bank CSV or PDF statements for free. Choose whether to sync your records privately across devices.

1. Get started and sign in

  1. Website: open MoneyMate NZ in your browser. It also works on a phone without installing an app.
  2. Android: install the test APK from a successful Android CI build if you are a tester. Download its build artifact, extract the APK, and install only if you trust the source. This is a test build, not a Google Play release. Do not uninstall your existing app or clear its data to update it without first backing up your records.
  3. Open Account and sign in with Google (if the button is available) or your email and password. For a new email account, choose Create account and complete email verification if prompted.
  4. You can also use a separate local guest profile without signing in, but guest records are kept on that device and cannot automatically follow your account.

2. Add income and expenses

  1. Open Home or Transactions.
  2. Select + Add income or + Add expense.
  3. Enter a category (such as Groceries), optional note, amount in NZD and date.
  4. Press Save on the website or in the Android entry form. The transaction appears in the history and monthly summary.

You can search the transaction history and create repeating income or expense schedules. Recurring transactions are recorded as they become due when you next open the app or website.

3. Import your NZD bank CSV or PDF statement for free

No bank connection, paid provider or bank password needed. You download a statement yourself from your bank and select it locally in MoneyMate NZ. Experimental PDF support is available for formats that can be parsed reliably. ANZ NZ statements with Withdrawals, Deposits and Balance columns can be checked using the statement dates and running balances; other NZ banks are accepted only when their PDF layout is unambiguous.
  1. Visit your bank's official website or app and download your transactions as a CSV or PDF statement.
  2. Website: open Bank connections → Choose bank CSV file or Choose bank PDF file. Transactions also has both import choices.
  3. Android: open Banking → Import NZD bank statement CSV or Import NZD bank statement PDF. Transactions and Export also show these options.
  4. Choose the downloaded file. Enter a short nickname such as Everyday or Savings—not your actual account number. Use the same nickname for future CSVs from the same account.
  5. Review the preview: MoneyMate suggests categories and reports how many new and previously imported transactions it recognises. For PDF, check every extracted amount, date and income/expense direction against your original statement; scanned pages may be misread. Confirm to add the new rows. Existing records are not replaced.
NZD only for now. The current budget totals use NZ dollars, so AUD statements are not supported. Duplicate detection is best-effort: two genuinely identical transactions may be indistinguishable. Review the preview and keep a JSON backup before a big import. Import does not automatically download new bank transactions every day. Not every ANZ, ASB, BNZ, Westpac, Kiwibank, TSB, SBS, Co-operative Bank, Heartland, Rabobank or other NZ bank PDF format is verified. See the NZ bank register. Never publicly share an unredacted statement.

4. Manage budgets, goals and bills

SectionWhat to do
BudgetsSet a monthly NZD limit for Groceries, Transport, Housing or another category. The spending bar shows your progress.
GoalsCreate a savings target and tap Add savings as you set money aside. This tracks your goal; it does not move money between bank accounts.
BillsEnter a bill name, amount and due date. Tap Record as paid to add a Bills expense and remove it from upcoming bills. It does not actually pay the bill.
HomeReview your monthly available balance, income, expenses, spending categories and upcoming/overdue bills.

5. Earn MoneyMate Points

  1. Sign in to your MoneyMate NZ account.
  2. Open Points & rewards on the website, or Rewards on Android.
  3. Tap Claim 10 points once per UTC day and use Refresh to see your current balance.

Your points total belongs to your signed-in account, so the same balance can appear on both devices even if financial cloud sync is turned off. Points are participation markers only: they cannot be cashed out, transferred or redeemed and have no money value.

6. Sync Android and website records (optional)

  1. On each device, open Export & backup or Export and save a full JSON backup first. Keep the files somewhere private.
  2. Sign in using the same Google/email account on both devices.
  3. On your main device, open Account → Enable secure cloud sync and approve storage of your account's records in Supabase. Wait for confirmation.
  4. On the other device, open Account → Enable secure cloud sync. If it has no financial entries, it can download the saved records. If it already has different entries, MoneyMate should stop and report a conflict instead of silently deleting them.
  5. Use Account → Sync now (website) or Sync records now (Android) when you want to check immediately. Normal updates sync automatically while online once enabled.
Never choose Restore, Use cloud copy or Clear storage to fix a sync conflict unless you have saved independent JSON backups first. Cloud sync is optional and real two-device testing is still recommended. Data from two independently edited offline devices may require manual conflict resolution.

7. Save a backup and export data

Website: open Export & backup → Save full JSON backup to save all supported finance records for the current profile. Use Export transactions CSV for a spreadsheet-compatible transaction export.

Android: open Export → Backup all data (JSON) or Export CSV and choose where to save it.

Restoring JSON replaces the selected profile's existing transactions, budgets, goals and bills. It is different from bank CSV import, which appends new transactions. Save an additional backup before restoring.

8. Help, safety and common questions

Why are my web transactions not showing on Android?

Confirm that both devices are signed into the same account and have enabled secure cloud sync in Account. Check the sync status on both. If MoneyMate reports a conflict, save both JSON backups and do not replace one copy blindly.

Why can't I connect a bank automatically?

Live automatic bank connections through Akahu (NZ) or Basiq (Australia) still require provider access, consent and security checks. The free manual CSV importer and experimental PDF importer are the current alternatives.

My bank file will not import. What should I check?

For CSV, use column headings (Date, Amount, Description or Date, Debit, Credit, Details), NZD, settled transactions and a file under 2 MB. For PDF, select the original PDF file (up to 4 MB). Text-based statement tables with clear plus/minus or DR/CR directions work best; complex debit/credit layouts may be rejected. Android also attempts on-device OCR for scanned pages. Never change a PDF filename to .csv. Dates may be YYYY-MM-DD or DD/MM/YYYY. Do not upload bank statements to a public issue to request help.

Will I stay logged in after refreshing?

Account sessions are designed to persist across refreshes. If you are unexpectedly signed out, check whether private browsing, blocked local storage or cleared app/site data is involved. Report it without including account details or login tokens.

How can I get the newest Android app?

Open Android CI, select a successful run from the main branch and download its moneymate-android-build artifact. Extract the ZIP and install the debug APK for testing. The release bundle is unsigned, not a public Play Store listing. Back up existing data first.

Read the privacy policy and account deletion information. Public bug reports can go to GitHub Issues without passwords, bank details, CSV files or financial exports.
